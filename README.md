# Hr-Analytics-Dashboard
An interactive Power BI dashboard built to analyze employee attrition patterns and workforce composition using an HR analytics dataset sourced from Kaggle. The dashboard enables HR stakeholders to identify attrition drivers across departments, salary bands, job roles, and demographic segments, supporting more informed retention strategies.

Project Overview

Employee attrition is a critical concern for organizations, as it directly impacts productivity, morale, and recruitment costs. This project transforms raw HR data into a consolidated, interactive dashboard that highlights key attrition trends and workforce metrics at a glance, with the ability to filter insights by age group.

Dataset

* Source: Kaggle (HR Analytics dataset)
* Records: 1,417 employees
* Key fields: Department, Job Role, Salary Slab, Age, Gender, Total Experience, Job Satisfaction, Attrition Status


Key Metrics

* Total Employees-1,417
* Active Employees-1,186
* Attrition Count-113
* Attrition Rate-19.2%
* Average Age-30.97
* Average Experience-5.95 years

Dashboard Features


* Age Group Filter — Toggle between 26-35, 36-45, 46-55, and 55+ segments to view attrition behavior across different life stages.
* Attrition by Department — Donut chart breaking down attrition share across Sales, Operations, HR, Marketing, and other departments.
* Attrition by Salary Slab — Comparison of attrition vs. retention across salary bands (0-3 LPA, 3-6 LPA, 6-10 LPA, 10+ LPA).
* Attrition by Job Role & Job Satisfaction — Cross-tabulated matrix showing attrition counts across satisfaction levels (1-4) for each job role.
* Age Group Distribution — Bar chart of employee count by age bracket.
* Attrition by Gender — Pie chart comparing male vs. female attrition share.
* Attrition Trend by Experience — Line chart tracking attrition against years of total experience.
* Department-wise Employee Count — Horizontal bar breakdown of headcount by department.


Key Insights


* Sales and Operations departments together account for the majority (83%) of total attrition, making them priority areas for retention efforts.
* The 6-10 LPA salary band shows the highest employee concentration among those analyzed, indicating salary-tier-specific attrition patterns worth deeper investigation.
* Job satisfaction scores of 1 and 3 appear frequently among high-attrition roles such as Laboratory Technician and Sales Executive, suggesting a link between satisfaction levels and turnover.
* Male employees show a slightly higher attrition share (59%) compared to female employees (41%).
* Attrition is not linear with experience — spikes appear at multiple points along the experience curve rather than concentrating solely among early-career employees.
